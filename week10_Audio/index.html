Clock: Audioreactive version

Week 10

This sketch extends my earlier Clock assignment. In the original, a new layer of translucent paper is added every hour, and the colors shift from blue to yellow to purple to navy as the day goes on. A small light particle also appears every second.

What I added for this assignment:

  • Microphone input using p5.AudioIn
  • Amplitude analysis using p5.Amplitude
  • Three relationships between the sound and the visuals (described below)

My audio source is the microphone. I used p5.Amplitude and getLevel() to measure how loud the sound is. Since the values are very small, I used map() to turn them into a 0–1 range, constrain() to keep the value from going over 1, and lerp() to smooth it so the animation doesn’t look jittery.

How the visual responds to the audio

  • The louder the sound, the bigger the paper layers become.
  • The louder the sound, the faster and farther the layers float up and down.
  • The louder the sound, the more sparkles appear on the screen.

When it is quiet, the clock looks calm and almost still. When someone speaks or makes noise, the layers swell and drift, and the sparkles fill the space.

The original Clock is about time slowly piling up, one layer per hour so it was very subtle. I wanted the microphone to make that quiet, slow piece feel more alive, so I connected every layer to the same sound. When someone speaks, all the layers react together, as if the whole day is responding to a voice at once.
I chose loudness (amplitude) because it is the most direct and easy to notice property of sound, and it matches the paper well. A louder sound is a bigger “presence,” so making the paper grow larger felt natural. I also made the layers float faster and farther, because I imagined sound as air moving through thin paper. A soft sound only makes the paper drift gently, while a loud sound makes it sway more strongly.
The sparkles are my way of showing the energy of the sound that is not just movement. In the original, one sparkle appears each second like a tiny tick of the clock. In this version, a louder sound fills the screen with more sparkles, so the light feels like it is coming from the voice itself.
When the room is quiet, the clock stays calm and almost still, and when someone makes noise, it becomes lively. I wanted the contrast between those two states to be clear, and I wanted the piece to still work as a clock when no one is making any sound.
